Press the chips. The same hotel, the same guests, the same night. Count only the fuel burned and the electricity bought and it is a modest number. Count what the kitchen buys and what the bins hold and it nearly doubles. Nothing physical changed; the boundary did. That is the first rule the evidence taught us, and the rest of this page is what follows from it.

01

Divide by rooms, by guests, or by square metres and the verdict on luxury changes.

A luxury hotel emits 2.3 to 3.1 times a midscale one per occupied room-night, but only 1.4 to 1.5 times per square metre. Most of the "luxury premium" is space per guest, not waste per square metre. Occupancy pulls the other way: an empty hotel is dirtier per night, so a two-star at 30% occupancy out-emits a five-star at 50%. Drag each archetype's occupancy; the bars re-sort. Then change the denominator.

Puig et al. 2017 (14 Spanish coastal hotels, per overnight stay: 2★ 23, 5★ 22, 4★ 12 kg CO₂e at 30 / 50 / 90–100% occupancy); CHSB 2025 (US luxury 32.1 vs upper-midscale 14.0 per occupied room; 1.43× per m²); Xuchao et al. 2010 on denominators. The archetypes are stylised: a fixed load that does not care whether rooms sell, plus a variable load that does.

02

The factor you pick for electricity moves the number more than anything you can do to the building.

eGRID's New York City factor is 0.392 kg per kWh. Local Law 97 prescribes 0.289 for compliance now and 0.145 from 2030. Same kilowatt-hours, three footprints, a 36 to 50% swing. The best-measured operational lever in the evidence base, occupancy-based guestroom controls, saves 0.3 to 1.0 kg a night. 03

Walk through the hotel. Every floor is a slice; every slice has a lever with a measured size.

Benchmarks give every room-night the same footprint whether or not the guest swam. Area-based allocation moves the figure by 0 to 5%; no large benchmark regression even has an amenity term. So the amenity question is answered from the bottom up: pool water surface × kWh per m², kilograms of linen × kWh per kg, covers × kWh per cover. 04

In 2030 the cap falls 61%, the electricity coefficient falls 50%, and gas gets nothing.

Local Law 97 caps a hotel at 106 kg CO₂e per m² until 2029, then 57 (or 41 under the property-type series), then 28, then 16. Every tonne over costs $268. Ninety percent of Manhattan hotels already exceed the 2030 cap. A gas-heated hotel cannot ride the grid down; a heat pump with a COP above 1.44 beats gas on the LL97 coefficient from day one. Scrub the year. Decide when heat and hot water go electric, how much demand you cut, how much Zone J renewable power you buy.

Local Law 97 of 2019; 1 RCNY 103-14. R-1 caps 0.00987 and 0.00526 tCO₂e/ft²; ESPM hotel 0.003850668, 0.002640017, 0.001465772. Manhattan LL84 CY2024, n=448. Theory insights I5–I7. Electrification at COP 3 is an assumption.

05

Once food is inside the boundary it is the largest line, and the menu is the largest lever.

Four Irish hotels: food and beverage procurement was 40 to 58% of a full-scope room-night, the largest category at every property. The Dolder Grand in Zürich: 48 to 56%. A beef main carries 8 to 11 times the footprint of a vegetarian one; a buffet wastes three times what plated service does. Build a dinner. Switch the factor set. Change the service.

06

What a hotel may say about all this changes on a known date.

From 27 September 2026 the EU bans claims that a product is climate-neutral on the strength of offsets bought outside the value chain. Courts got there first: KLM (Amsterdam, March 2024), Eurowings, the German Federal Court's klimaneutral ruling. Only 6% of REDD+ credits in one study were tied to real reductions. SBTi says credits do not count toward targets at all. Slide the date; click a claim.
