Hotel Carbon Evidence Map

What the research graph has established so far about the carbon footprint of a luxury hotel stay and its amenities, organised the way the graph is: six facet goals, the options weighed and decisions taken under each, the observations that carry the numbers, and the revisits where earlier conclusions were corrected. The case property is the renovated Waldorf Astoria New York. The ideas are presented as an interactive essay at bobbby.online/hotel-carbon. Two companion tools use these figures: Hotel Carbon Studio (knobs for the property and its amenities; results sliced by amenity, end use, scope and carrier) and Hotel Carbon Storylines (dated events and grid paths to 2040, Monte Carlo bands, stories compared, theories recorded). The theory track's rules, guidelines, intervention library and hypotheses are being written in the project's theory/ notes.

    Still unverified

    Figures that circulate in the hotel-carbon literature but could not be read in a primary text during verification. They are carried in the graph with that status, not dropped.

    • Deng & Burnett 2000, 564 kWh/m²/yr. The publisher abstract gives no number; the value is consistent across two peer-reviewed citing papers (Priyadarsini 2009, Filimonau 2011) but was not read in the original.
    • Bohdanowicz & Martinac 2007 per-guest-night kWh. 184 Hilton and Scandic hotels; the abstract reports determinants only, and no open copy of the full text exists.
    • Trianti-Stourna 1998, 1,094.5 kWh/m² of water surface. Not found in any accessible text. Kampel 2013/2016 read the paper and report 4,300 (Mediterranean) and 5,200 (continental) kWh/m² WS as unsourced literature values, and 450.1 kWh/m² of floor area as the audited Greek sample mean.
    • Kampel 2016 kWh per visitor statistics (mean 26, median about 22, range 10–80) come from Smedegård 2021, not from the 2016 abstract or thesis text searched.
    • Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius 2008 reuse rates (35.1% → 44.1% → 49.3%) are not in the abstract; the full text is paywalled.
    • Point, Tyedmers & Naugler 2012 kg CO2e per bottle. Functional unit confirmed as one 750 mL bottle; the number itself was not read.
    • Rosselló-Batle 2010 per-night values (20–31 kg CO2e per guest-night; 650/720 MJ/m²) are known only through Attari & Hanrahan 2026 and Filimonau 2011.
    • Spa treatment rooms and commercial hot tubs have no measured hotel energy benchmark in the literature reached; only residential hot-tub data and an unsourced "up to 35% of hotel energy for additional services" claim exist.

    How this page was built

    Companion tools: Hotel Carbon Studio and Hotel Carbon Storylines.

    The graph lives in the deciduous decision-graph tool. Each researcher logged a verified source as an observation under the facet goal it informs; approaches were logged as options, the choice between them as a decision with chosen and rejected edges, and each facet's closing state as an outcome. A revisit is attached to the node it corrects.

    Bibliographic records were resolved through Crossref and OpenAlex; abstracts came from Europe PMC, CORE, or Wayback Machine captures of publisher pages where the live site refuses automated access; full texts were read from open repository copies where they exist. Titles on this page are the node titles as logged, so each carries its number and unit. The companion notes files (01 to 07) in the project hold the verbatim abstracts and table references.
